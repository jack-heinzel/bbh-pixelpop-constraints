\section{Introduction}

With more than 250 \ac{BBH} mergers now cataloged by the LIGO \cite{}, Virgo \cite{}, and KAGRA \cite{} detectors \cite{}, we are beginning to reveal the structure of the \ac{BBH} population. The \ac{BH} mass distribution has a prominent peak at $\sim 10\Msun$, a feature at $\sim 30$--$35 \Msun$ which may be a local peak or steepening \citep{}, and a tail that extends into the theorized \ac{PISN} mass gap \cite{}. Most \ac{BH} spins are small \cite{}, though there exists a subpopulation of rapid spins \cite{}, and the astrophysical \ac{BBH} merger rate increases with redshift. 
The population of \ac{BBH} is \textit{correlated} as well. The effective spin \cite{} distribution appears to broaden with mass ratio, redshift, and appears to have a nontrivial narrow-broad-narrow-broad structure across the mass distribution \cite{} \jh{fix that sentence}. 

\jh{maybe something about astrophysics...?}
Most of population results come from models that assume the population is uncorrelated or let a single pair of binary parameters correlate while holding the rest to a fixed, usually parametric, form. The $q$--$\chieff$ correlation, for example, is typically measured with a mass distribution that does not depend on spin, and the mass spectrum with a single mass ratio distribution at every primary mass. Each conclusion is therefore conditional on assumptions that other analyses relax. A fully joint model is computationally demanding, and for the smaller catalogs of a few dozen events, correlations among all the parameters at once were too subtle to detect \citep[e.g., ][]{Biscoveanu:2022qac}. With hundreds of \acp{BBH}, this is no longer true.

Here we fit the masses, mass ratio and spins jointly, letting each distribution correlate in an unspecified, flexible manner \jh{also exploring redshift dependence, though chains not done so need to wait for conclusions}. Towards equal mass ratios, the primary mass spectrum peaks at $m_1 = \jointTenMuFlex\,\Msun$ and $\jointThirtyFiveMuFlex\,\Msun$ (mean $q = \jointTenQMeanFlex$ and $\jointThirtyFiveQMeanFlex$, but consistent with \textit{peaking} at $q\to 1$). The mean mass ratio falls away from these peaks, and at $q \sim 0.5$ we find \jh{99\%ish} evidence for a second pair of peaks, at $m_1 = \jointTwentyMuFlex\,\Msun$ and $\jointSixtyMuFlex\,\Msun$ (mean $q = \jointTwentyQMeanFlex$ and $\jointSixtyQMeanFlex$), about twice the masses of the first. The spin behavior splits the same way: the near-equal-mass peaks have a narrow effective spin distribution, while the $q \sim 0.5$ peaks have a broad, nearly symmetric one, as expected from large, isotropically oriented spins.

We also show that the separation into two families of peaks with different mass ratios and spin properties also explains the broadening $\chieff$ with mass ratio, a correlation identified in \citet{LIGOScientific:2026ctl}, and can explain the observed broadening with redshift \cite{Biscoveanu:2022qac} as a confounding variable effect \jh{double check, but I think this is be right. Think a bit more about this}. These mass peaks appear remarkably consistent with a dynamical first generation and a hierarchical, second generation of \ac{BBH} mergers, and we show the observed mass ranges are consistent with the expected remnant masses from \ac{GR}. 

The $\sim10\Msun$ peak does show a statistically significant preference for $\chieff > 0$, however, and the $\sim \featTwenty\,\Msun$ peak is slightly more massive than the typical remnants from $\sim \featTen\,\Msun$ peak \ac{BBH}. This suggests that not \textit{all} $\sim \featTen\,\Msun$ \ac{BBH} are assembled dynamically, or that \jh{more complex dynamical theories are required, like that "not all dynamical bhs have isotropic spins paper from Davide's group", or dynamical environments evolve over cosmic time, perhaps 2G mergers today had higher z progenitors which had higher masses, e.g., $\featTen\,\Msun$ peak drifts a bit over cosmic time... something something metallicity and winds} the dynamical environments responsible for the $\sim \featTwenty\,\Msun$ peak are at a different evolutionary period from the $\sim \featTen\,\Msun$ dynamical mergers.

We infer the population of \acp{BBH} following \ac{GWTC-5},  using the same dataset of 259 \acp{BBH} with \ac{FAR} $<1\peryear$ and sensitivity estimates as described in \citet{LIGOScientific:2026ctl}. We use the hierarchical Bayesian inference framework, with details described in the supplemental material.

We consider a pair of flexible models which model the joint distribution of primary mass $m_1$, mass ratio $q$, and effective aligned and precessing spins $\chieff$ and $\chip$, respectively. We decompose the distribution as
\begin{equation}
    p(m_1, q, \chieff, \chip) = p(m_1, q)p(\chieff, \chip \mid m_1, q),
    \label{eq: spins conditional on mass basic model}
\end{equation}
with an uncorrelated redshift distribution that evolves as a powerlaw \jh{we also do other stuff more sophisticated to see if redshift matters}.

The first model we present is a four-dimensional, nonparametric model for the correlated space of primary mass, mass ratio, and the effective aligned spin $\chieff$ and effective precessing spin $\chip$. Specifically, we build a nonparametric model for $p(m_1, q)$ using a Mat\'ern field in \PixelPop \cite{}, and construct a correlated conditional model for $p(\chieff, \chip \mid m_1, q)$ using a separate Mat\'ern field, details in the supplemental material \jh{to do!}. We model the comoving merger rate as evolving according to a simple powerlaw in redshift \cite[e.g.][]{Fishbach:2018edt}, uncorrelated with masses or spins. \jh{models for redshift correlation running.} 

Our second model is a bivariate Gaussian in $(\chieff, \chip)$,
\begin{equation}
    p(\chieff, \chip \mid m_1, q) = \mathcal{N}\big(\vec{\mu}, C\big),
\end{equation}
whose means $\mu_{\chieff}, \mu_{\chip}$, standard deviations $\sigma_{\chieff}, \sigma_{\chip}$ and correlation coefficient $\rho$ are each Mat\'ern fields over $(m_1, q)$ (explicit form in Eq.~\eqref{eq: gaussian cov} of the Supplemental Material), truncated to the effective spins physical domain (Eq.~12 of \citet{iwaya_analytic_2025}). \jh{we also test some other models where we say each Gaussian parameter can vary with mass, mass ratio, redshift, but as a separable, additive evolution like in Sylvia's paper. TBD on how those look.} 

Though a Gaussian model may be misspecified against the astrophysical shape of the $\chieff, \chip$ population, Gaussian models are attractive for their interpretative abilities. We may directly interpret the measured moments against their prior. The \flexiblespins model allows for generic, non-Gaussian spin distributions, but it induces a nontrivial prior on the implied moments (mean and standard deviation) of the spin distribution. For instance, it is plausible that the \flexiblespins model preferentially broadens in regions which are poorly constrained by the data, as the model reverts to the preferentially-smooth field prior. Data-poor regions in the \gaussianspins model, by contrast, should correspond to broader \textit{uncertainties} on the moments, as opposed to \textit{biases}. \jh{In practice, we observe similar constraints on the population moments for both models.}

\subsection{The \featTen $\Msun$ feature}

We find the global peak of the \ac{BH} mass spectrum at $m_1 = \peakTenMuFlex\,\Msun$, with a narrow width $\sigma = \peakTenSigmaFlex\,\Msun$ (Table~\ref{tab: peaks}).\jh{too many sig figs again} The \featTen $\Msun$ feature has been known since catalogs were a few dozens of events ~\cite[e.g.,][]{LIGOScientific:2020kqk, Talbot:2018cva, Tiwari:2020otp, Edelman:2021zkw, KAGRA:2021duu, Callister:2023tgi}, and its prominence has only grown more significant with larger catalogs \cite{LIGOScientific:2025pvj, LIGOScientific:2026ctl}. %Because it sits just above the low-mass turn-on of the \ac{BH} mass spectrum, its precise shape is partly degenerate with how that turn-on is modeled~\cite{Talbot:2018cva, LIGOScientific:2020kqk, Farah:2021qom}. 
Binaries in the \featTen $\Msun$ feature are strongly near-equal mass: in $m_1 \in [8, 12]\,\Msun$, the mode of the mass ratio distribution is $\qModeWindowTenFlex$ and its mean is $\langle q \rangle = \qWindowTenFlex$, and the feature is absent from the $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$ slice in Fig.~\ref{fig: mass peaks}. This is consistent with~\cite{Farah:2023swu, LIGOScientific:2026ctl} \todo{add Flanagan et al.\ 2026 (arXiv:2606.14472): pairing $\beta \gtrsim 2$ for $m_1 \lesssim 18\,\Msun$; not yet in main.bib}, and in mild tension with the mass ratio distribution peaking away from unity found by \citet{Godfrey:2023oxb}, \citet{Sadiq:2023zee} and \citet{Sridhar:2025kvi}, and in the Isolated Peak model of \citet{LIGOScientific:2025pvj}. \todo{might want to make a plot of $p(q \mid m_1 \approx 10\,\Msun)$ for a future referee question}

The spins are small and preferentially aligned with the orbit: the effective spin distribution is narrow, with standard deviation \red{$\sigma_{\chieff} = \peakSpinTenEffSdCoreFlex$ ($\peakSpinTenEffSdCoreGauss$ for the \gaussianspins model, here and below in parentheses) in $m_1 \in [8, 12]\,\Msun$, $q \in [0.8, 1]$ (core $|\chieff| < 0.8$, as in Fig.~\ref{fig: chi_eff width}; $\peakSpinTenEffSdCorePriorFlex$ under the prior)} and a mean, \red{$\mu_{\chieff} = \peakSpinTenEffMeanCoreFlex$ ($\peakSpinTenEffMeanCoreGauss$), positive with probability $\peakSpinTenPEffPosFlex\%$ ($\peakSpinTenPEffPosGauss\%$)}, that is small but positive (Fig.~\ref{fig: spin peaks}) \jh{sharpness of 10M peak and maybe sharpness of Xeff is limited by MC?}.

The precessing spin is constrained at the \featTen\,\Msun feature more tightly than anywhere else in the population. For $q \in [0.8, 1]$, \red{the population mean of $\chip$ in $m_1 \in [8, 12]\,\Msun$ is $\peakSpinTenPMeanFlex$ ($\peakSpinTenPMeanGauss$) and its standard deviation $\peakSpinTenPSdFlex$ ($\peakSpinTenPSdGauss$), compared with $\peakSpinTenPMeanPriorFlex$ and $\peakSpinTenPSdPriorFlex$ under the prior}. Both are among the smallest values across the mass spectrum: the mean $\chip$ increases with primary mass, and the unequal-mass \featTwenty and \featSixty\,\Msun features prefer larger and broader $\chip$ distributions (Fig.~\ref{fig: spin peaks}; SM). Together with the narrow $\chieff$ distribution, this implies that black holes in this feature have small spin magnitudes overall, not only small aligned components. It also limits the contribution of binaries with large, isotropically oriented spins, such as second-generation black holes with $\chi \approx 0.7$, which would produce $\chip \sim 0.5$~\cite{Gerosa:2021mno} \todo{check this number}. This constraint is mostly an upper limit. Precession is harder to measure for near-equal-mass binaries, where the in-plane spin is a smaller fraction of the orbital angular momentum~\cite{Apostolatos:1994mx, Green:2020ptm}, and $\chip$ is positive by construction and poorly measured for individual events, so populations with small and with zero in-plane spins are difficult to distinguish~\cite{Vitale:2022dpa, Miller:2024sui}. We therefore do not read the nonzero lower bound on the mean of $\chip$ as evidence for misaligned spins, which would constrain natal kicks in the isolated channel~\cite{Kalogera:1999tq, Gerosa:2018wbw, Callister:2020vyz} \todo{check whether the lower bound persists for a simulated population with zero in-plane spin}.

The origin of the peak is debated: a pile-up near $10\,\Msun$ may be set by core-collapse physics, independent of the binary formation channel \todo{cite Schneider et al.\ 2021 (A\&A 645, A5) and 2023 (ApJL 950, L9) on explodability; neither is in main.bib yet}, and models of young star clusters also produce many mergers at these masses~\cite{DiCarlo:2019pmf, Santoliquido:2020bry, Mapelli:2021gyv}. Near-equal masses with small, aligned spins are, however, the expected signature of isolated binary evolution~\cite{Zaldarriaga:2017qkw, Qin:2018vaa, Fuller:2019sxi, Bavera:2020inc, Zevin:2022wrw}, and we take this feature to be dominated by such first-generation binaries.

\subsection{The \featTwenty $\Msun$ feature}

For mass ratios $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$, the primary mass spectrum has a peak at $m_1 = \peakTwentyMuFlex\,\Msun$ with width $\sigma = \peakTwentySigmaFlex\,\Msun$ (Table~\ref{tab: peaks}). Unlike the \featTen and \featThirtyFive\,\Msun features, binaries in this feature are unequal mass: the mean mass ratio in $m_1 \in [15, 25]\,\Msun$ is $\langle q \rangle = \qWindowTwentyFlex$, and the feature does not appear in the $q \in [0.8, 1]$ slice of Fig.~\ref{fig: mass peaks}. Its location does not depend on our choice of mass ratio slice: a joint fit to $p(m_1, q)$ with no mass ratio range imposed places the peak at $\jointTwentyMuFlex\,\Msun$, with mean mass ratio $\jointTwentyQMeanFlex$ (SM). Within $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$, the merger rate at the peak exceeds a local power law across $m_1 \in [15, 30]\,\Msun$ with posterior probability $\peakTwentyExcessPLocFlex\%$\red{, against $\peakTwentyExcessPLocPriorFlex\%$ under the prior. $\peakTwentyNEvents$ events have median $m_1 \in [15, 25]\,\Msun$ and median $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$ ($\peakTwentyNEventsPE$ by their summed posterior probability in this region)}.

The spins of binaries in this feature also differ from those at the near-equal-mass features. The effective spin distribution is broad and nearly symmetric about zero, and larger precessing spins are preferred (Fig.~\ref{fig: spin peaks}). This is the broadening of the $\chieff$ distribution toward unequal mass ratios seen across the population: averaged over primary mass, the core width of the $\chieff$ distribution is $\sigma_{\chieff} = \chiQBroadSdQLoCoreFlex$ at $q = 0.35$, against $\chiQBroadSdQHiCoreFlex$ at $q = 0.85$ (Fig.~\ref{fig: chi_eff width q}), consistent with the mass ratio dependence of $\chieff$ found by parametric models~\cite{Callister:2021fpo, Adamcewicz:2023mov, LIGOScientific:2026ctl}. \red{In $m_1 \in [16, 22]\,\Msun$, $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$, $\sigma_{\chieff} = \peakSpinTwentyEffSdCoreFlex$ ($\peakSpinTwentyEffSdCoreGauss$) and $\mu_{\chieff} = \peakSpinTwentyEffMeanCoreFlex$ ($\peakSpinTwentyEffMeanCoreGauss$) (core $|\chieff| < 0.8$), against $\sigma_{\chieff} = \peakSpinTenEffSdCoreFlex$ at the \featTen\,\Msun feature.}

Earlier analyses with weakly parameterized models reported hints of additional structure near $20\,\Msun$~\cite{Tiwari:2020otp, Tiwari:2021yvr, Edelman:2022ydv, Toubiana:2023egi, MaganaHernandez:2024uty}, and \citet{LIGOScientific:2025pvj} noted a possible third feature there in \ac{GWTC-4}, although the data did not require it. Using \PixelPop, \citet{LIGOScientific:2026ctl} found no evidence for such a feature in the \ac{GWTC-5} primary mass distribution. \red{Over all mass ratios, the rate at the feature still exceeds a local power law with $P_{\rm loc} = \peakTwentyExcessPLocAllQFlex\%$, against $\peakTwentyExcessPLocFlex\%$ within $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$.} In the marginal distribution, the feature is weak because it sits between the much larger \featTen and \featThirtyFive\,\Msun features; it becomes clear only once the mass ratio is resolved. As discussed for the \featThirtyFive\,\Msun feature, it also fills in the rise toward $\featThirtyFive\,\Msun$ in the marginal distribution. A subpopulation of rapidly spinning black holes near $16\,\Msun$ has also been proposed~\cite{Plunkett:2026pxt} \todo{cite Farah et al.\ 2026 and Tong et al.\ 2025a, as cited in GWTC-5 Sec.~4.4.1; check they are in main.bib}; the feature we find here is identified by its mass ratio, independent of the spins.

The location, mass ratio and spins of this feature are what one expects of second-generation mergers built from the \featTen\,\Msun feature. Two $\sim \featTen\,\Msun$ black holes merge into a remnant of $\sim 18\,\Msun$ with spin $\chi \approx 0.7$; if the remnant merges again with a first-generation black hole from the same peak, the binary has $q \approx 0.5$, and if it is assembled dynamically, its spins are isotropically oriented, giving a broad, symmetric $\chieff$ distribution~\cite{Fishbach:2017dwv, Gerosa:2017kvu, Rodriguez:2019huv, Gerosa:2021mno}. We test this in Sec.~\ref{sec: relationships}, where we find the feature sits slightly above the expected location. We regard the peak itself as tentative: that binaries at these masses have unequal masses is well measured, but whether they form a peak rather than a broad unequal-mass component rests on relatively few events.

\subsection{The \featThirtyFive $\Msun$ feature}

A peak near $\featThirtyFive\,\Msun$ has been reported since the earliest population analyses~\cite{Talbot:2018cva, LIGOScientific:2018jsj, LIGOScientific:2020kqk, KAGRA:2021duu, Farah:2023vsc}, and more detailed analyses find that the binaries in this peak prefer near-equal masses~\cite{Roy:2025ktr, Sridhar:2025kvi}.


We emphasize that the mass spectrum for $q\in [0.8, 1]$ appears to prefer a \textit{peaked} $\featThirtyFive\,\Msun$ feature rather than the steepening of an already decreasing power law continuum. This shape has previously been uncertain: \citet{LIGOScientific:2025pvj} found that the marginal primary mass distribution was consistent with either a peak or a steepening, but could not distinguish which, and using the latest \ac{GWTC-5} dataset, \citet{LIGOScientific:2026ctl} indicated that the behavior across the $\featThirtyFive\,\Msun$ feature was more consistent with a steepening powerlaw slope. We observe this same behavior in the marginal primary mass distribution: the merger rate in $m_1 \in [30, 35]\,\Msun$ is consistent with that in $[25, 30]\,\Msun$, but larger than that in $[35, 40]\,\Msun$ (Table~\ref{tab: peak odds}). For $q \in [0.8, 1]$, however, the merger rate rises into the feature as well as falling out of it, with posterior probability $P_{\rm peak} = \peakOddsHiPFlex\%$ for a peak, against $P_{\rm peak}^{\rm prior} = \peakOddsHiPPriorFlex\%$ under the prior (posterior-to-prior odds $\mathcal{O}_{\rm peak} = \peakOddsHiBFFlex$); marginalized over all mass ratios, the odds drop to $\mathcal{O}_{\rm peak} = \peakOddsAllBFFlex$. The difference comes from the low-mass side of the feature: the $\featTwenty\,\Msun$ feature populates the region between $\featTen$ and $\featThirtyFive\,\Msun$ at unequal mass ratios, filling in the rise toward $\featThirtyFive\,\Msun$ in the marginal distribution. We argue this clarifies the degeneracy identified by \citet{LIGOScientific:2025pvj}: the steepening seen in the marginal distribution is what a near-equal-mass peak looks like once the unequal-mass binaries from the \featTwenty $\Msun$ feature are averaged in.

\subsection{The \featSixty $\Msun$ feature}

The second unequal-mass feature is at $m_1 = \peakSixtyMuFlex\,\Msun$, with width $\sigma = \peakSixtySigmaFlex\,\Msun$ (Table~\ref{tab: peaks}). Like the \featTwenty\,\Msun feature, it appears in the $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$ slice of Fig.~\ref{fig: mass peaks} but not in the near-equal-mass slice, and the mean mass ratio in $m_1 \in [50, 70]\,\Msun$ is $\langle q \rangle = \qWindowSixtyFlex$. It is the least well measured of the four features. A joint fit to $p(m_1, q)$ with no mass ratio range imposed finds a peak in a fraction $\jointSixtyPeakFracFlex\%$ of posterior draws, at $\jointSixtyMuFlex\,\Msun$ with mean mass ratio $\jointSixtyQMeanFlex$ (SM), and within $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$ the merger rate at the peak exceeds a local power law across $m_1 \in [40, 100]\,\Msun$ with posterior probability $\peakSixtyExcessPLocFlex\%$\red{, against $\peakSixtyExcessPLocPriorFlex\%$ under the prior. $\peakSixtyNEvents$ events have median $m_1 \in [50, 70]\,\Msun$ and median $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$ ($\peakSixtyNEventsPE$ by their summed posterior probability in this region)}.

As at the \featTwenty\,\Msun feature, the effective spin distribution is broad and nearly symmetric (Fig.~\ref{fig: spin peaks}), and the width of the $\chieff$ distribution grows above $\sim 45\,\Msun$, driven by the unequal-mass binaries (Fig.~\ref{fig: chi_eff width}). \red{In $m_1 \in [55, 65]\,\Msun$, $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$, $\sigma_{\chieff} = \peakSpinSixtyEffSdCoreFlex$ ($\peakSpinSixtyEffSdCoreGauss$) and $\mu_{\chieff} = \peakSpinSixtyEffMeanCoreFlex$ ($\peakSpinSixtyEffMeanCoreGauss$) (core $|\chieff| < 0.8$). The mean $\chip$, $\peakSpinSixtyPMeanFlex$ ($\peakSpinSixtyPMeanGauss$), is narrower than under the prior ($\peakSpinSixtyPMeanPriorFlex$) but centred near it, so $\chip$ is only weakly informative here.} \citet{LIGOScientific:2026ctl} similarly found a subpopulation of rapidly spinning black holes, with spins $\chi \sim 0.7$, at primary masses above $\sim 45\,\Msun$, as well as at $\sim 10$--$20\,\Msun$.

Weakly parameterized analyses of \ac{GWTC-3} and \ac{GWTC-4} showed hints of a rise in the merger rate near $60\,\Msun$~\cite{MaganaHernandez:2024uty, LIGOScientific:2025pvj}, but \citet{LIGOScientific:2026ctl} found no evidence for a peak there in the \ac{GWTC-5} primary mass distribution. They did find a flatter mass ratio distribution above $m_1 \sim 40\,\Msun$, together with the change in spin properties, and noted that this is consistent with hierarchical mergers of a second-generation and a first-generation black hole, which would have $q \approx 0.5$. Our result is in line with this, and resolves it further: the unequal-mass binaries above $\sim 40\,\Msun$ are concentrated around $\featSixty\,\Msun$ rather than spread across the high-mass tail. \red{Over all mass ratios, the excess at the \featSixty\,\Msun feature has $P_{\rm loc} = \peakSixtyExcessPLocAllQFlex\%$, against $\peakSixtyExcessPLocFlex\%$ within $q \in [0.3, 0.65]$.}

A primary mass of $\sim \featSixty\,\Msun$ lies in the pair-instability mass gap, whose lower edge has been placed near $\sim 45\,\Msun$ using the secondary masses and spins of \ac{GWTC-4} binaries~\cite{Tong:2025wpz, Antonini:2025ilj}, though see~\cite{Ray:2025xti} \todo{check these three on the location and significance of the lower edge}. Such black holes cannot form directly from stellar collapse, but can be the remnants of earlier mergers. Two $\sim \featThirtyFive\,\Msun$ black holes merge into a remnant of $\sim 60\,\Msun$ with spin $\chi \approx 0.7$, and if this remnant merges again with a first-generation black hole from the \featThirtyFive\,\Msun feature, the binary has $q \approx 0.5$ and, if assembled dynamically, a broad, symmetric $\chieff$ distribution~\cite{Rodriguez:2019huv, Kimball:2020opk, Gerosa:2021mno, Antonini:2024het}. Hierarchical subpopulations at high mass have also been argued for in \ac{GWTC-4}~\cite{Plunkett:2026pxt, Li:2025iux}. In Sec.~\ref{sec: relationships}, we find that the location of the \featSixty\,\Msun feature matches the remnant of the \featThirtyFive\,\Msun feature, with $\sim 5\%$ of the mass radiated. As with the \featTwenty\,\Msun feature, we regard the peak itself as tentative.

\subsection*{Field priors}

\red{All fields are Mat\'ern Gaussian random fields built in the Laplacian eigenbasis of their grid (the stochastic-PDE construction of \PixelPop). On a $D$-dimensional grid with per-axis Laplacian eigenvectors $v^{(d)}_k$ and eigenvalues $\lambda^{(d)}_k$, a field is
\begin{gather}
    f = \sum_{\vec{k}} a_{\vec{k}}\, z_{\vec{k}} \prod_{d=1}^{D} v^{(d)}_{k_d}, \qquad z_{\vec{k}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1), \\
    a_{\vec{k}} \propto \Big(1 + \sum_{d} \frac{\ell_d^2 \lambda^{(d)}_{k_d}}{8\nu}\Big)^{-(\nu + D/2)/2},
\end{gather}
with ranges $\ell_d$ (in bins) along each axis and smoothness $\nu$; the amplitudes are normalized so that the site-averaged variance of $f$ is $s^2$, and the constant mode is set to zero. Unless stated otherwise, the hyperpriors are \PixelPop's: $\ln s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 2)$, $\ln \nu \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.5)$, and $\ln \ell_d \sim \mathcal{U}(\ln \ell_{\min}, \ln B_d)$, log-uniform from one bin ($\ell_{\min} = 1$) to the number of bins $B_d$ along the axis.
\begin{itemize}
\item Mass: $p(\ln m_1, q) \propto e^{L}$, with $L$ a 2D Mat\'ern field on a $100 \times 100$ grid in $\ln m_1 \in [\ln 3, \ln 200]$ and $q$, keeping all modes; hyperparameters $s$, $\nu$, $\ell_{\ln m_1}$, $\ell_q$. The rate is windowed to $m_2 > 3\,\Msun$.
\item \flexiblespins spins: $p(\chieff, \chip \mid m_1, q) \propto e^{F}$ on the physical window $\chip < \chi_{\rm p, max}(\chieff, q)$, on $100 \times 100$ bins over $[-1, 1] \times [0, 1]$, normalized separately at each $(m_1, q)$. $F$ is a 4D Mat\'ern field restricted to the lowest $k \times k$ ($k = 10$) eigenmodes along $\ln m_1$ and $q$ and all modes along $\chieff$ and $\chip$, with one $s$, one $\nu$ and four ranges. The spin ranges are bounded below by $0.05$ in $\chi$ (2.5 bins in $\chieff$, 5 in $\chip$). Modes that are constant in $\chi$ cancel in the normalization and are set to zero, so a zero field is uniform on the physical window: there is no isotropic or template mean.
\item Split spins (robustness): $F = G_0(\chi) + G_1(\ln m_1, q, \chi)$: $G_0$ is a mass-independent 2D Mat\'ern field with its own $(s_0, \nu_0, \ell)$; $G_1$ keeps only the mass-varying modes, with its own $(s_1, \nu_1)$ and a separable spectrum (a Mat\'ern spectrum over the mass modes times one over the spin modes), so that $s_1$ measures how much the spins change with mass. Same hyperpriors as above.
\item \gaussianspins spins: Each of the five parameters $\theta_i \in \{\mu_{\chieff}, \mu_{\chip}, \ln\sigma_{\chieff}, \ln\sigma_{\chip}, \operatorname{atanh}\rho\}$ is an offset plus a field, $\theta_i = c_i + f_i(\ln m_1, q)$, where $f_i$ is a 2D Mat\'ern field on the lowest $k \times k$ mass modes (mean mode zero) with its own amplitude $s_i$ and ranges, and a shared $\nu$. The offsets are uniform: $c_{\mu_{\chieff}} \in [-0.5, 0.5]$, $c_{\mu_{\chip}} \in [0, 1]$, $c_{\ln\sigma} \in [\ln 0.03, 0]$ for both widths, and $c_{\operatorname{atanh}\rho} \in [-2, 2]$. The amplitudes are $\ln s_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\ln 0.1, 1)$ for the two means and $\mathcal{N}(\ln 0.5, 1)$ for the two log-widths and $\operatorname{atanh}\rho$. The widths entering the Gaussian are $\sqrt{e^{2\ln\sigma} + 0.02^2}$, a soft floor at one spin bin. The Gaussian is truncated to the physical window and normalized at each $(m_1, q)$.
\item Redshift: A power law in $(1+z)$, uncorrelated with masses and spins, with its standard prior.
\end{itemize}}

\subsection*{Gaussian spin model}

In the Gaussian spin model, the conditional distribution of the effective spins is a bivariate Gaussian,
\begin{equation}
    p(\chieff, \chip \mid m_1, q) = \mathcal{N}\left[
    \vec{\mu}(m_1, q), 
    C(m_1, q)
    \right],
\end{equation}
with mean vector and covariance matrix
\begin{align}
    \vec{\mu}(m_1, q) &= \begin{bmatrix}
    \mu_{\chieff}(m_1, q) \\ 
    \mu_{\chip}(m_1, q)
    \end{bmatrix},
    \\
    C(m_1, q) &= \begin{bmatrix}
    \sigma^2_{\chieff}(m_1, q) & \rho(m_1, q)\sigma_{\chieff}(m_1, q)\sigma_{\chip}(m_1, q) \\ 
    \rho(m_1, q)\sigma_{\chieff}(m_1, q)\sigma_{\chip}(m_1, q) & \sigma^2_{\chip}(m_1, q)
    \end{bmatrix}, \label{eq: gaussian cov}
\end{align}
where each of $\mu_{\chieff}$, $\mu_{\chip}$, $\sigma_{\chieff}$, $\sigma_{\chip}$ and $\rho$ is its own Mat\'ern field over $(m_1, q)$. The distribution is truncated to the physical domain of $(\chieff, \chip)$, Eq.~12 of \citet{iwaya_analytic_2025}.
